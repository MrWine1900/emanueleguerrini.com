Biomass moisture changes the business case

Why water content changes usable output, fuel logistics and combustion operation.

Bioenergy
2026
EMANUELE GUERRINI

Biomass is not a uniform fuel. “Woodchips” is therefore not enough to specify an industrial combustion system. Moisture, particle size, ash and contaminants all determine how the plant behaves.

Moisture and useful energy

Higher moisture requires energy for evaporation during combustion. At equal as-received mass, lower heating value generally falls. More fuel mass may be needed to deliver the same useful thermal output.

Beyond efficiency

High or inconsistent moisture can complicate ignition, combustion stability, air control, furnace temperature and moving-grate operation. Available plant capacity should be checked across the expected fuel range, not only for an ideal sample.

Logistics and fuel quality

Wetter fuel means more tonnes handled per unit of useful energy. Storage, transport, feeding systems, auxiliary power and deterioration risks all matter. Defining minimum fuel acceptance criteria is an economic as well as a technical decision.

Structure the feasibility check

An initial assessment should include fuel laboratory data, moisture range, as-received LHV, annual operating hours, expected load-dependent efficiency, logistics cost and service requirements. Flue-gas dust loading and emissions control deserve specific attention.

Ask the right question

The key question is not simply “How many tonnes of chips will we burn?” but how much useful thermal energy can reliably be delivered over the year with the fuel actually available.

Bottom line. A robust biomass business case requires a fuel specification before the final boiler specification.
