The best boiler is not always the most efficient

Why full-load efficiency is only one element in a sound boiler selection.

Steam generation
2026
EMANUELE GUERRINI

Declared efficiency is an important parameter in comparing steam boilers. However, it does not fully describe the economics of a plant. Selection must reflect the demand profile and operation over the full year.

Part-load behaviour matters

A boiler may operate at rated load for only a small part of the year. Burner turndown, start-stop cycles and standby losses can make annual fuel use differ significantly from nominal-point comparisons.

Distinguish efficiencies

Combustion efficiency, boiler useful efficiency and whole-plant efficiency have different boundaries. Blowdown, condensate return, deaeration, feedwater temperature, steam distribution and electrical auxiliaries can influence the plant-level result.

Auxiliaries and maintenance

Economizers, conductivity control, chemical dosing, supervision and pump redundancy can improve operation, when properly specified. Maintenance access, spare parts, service coverage and downtime deserve equal consideration.

Compliance and project boundaries

Design pressure, temperature, applicable standards, PED category and local requirements should be agreed early. Apparent equivalents can differ in documentation, inspection, installation scope and contractual responsibilities.

Make comparisons comparable

Use a consistent comparison sheet covering capacity, hours by load band, forecast fuel consumption, supply boundaries, maintenance and total cost of ownership. Purchase price is only one part of the decision.

Bottom line. The aim is to deliver safe, reliable steam at the best whole-life cost for the process, not simply to choose the highest catalogue efficiency.
