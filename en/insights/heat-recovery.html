Economizers: five checks before you buy

A practical checklist for turning flue-gas temperature into a reliable design brief.

Heat recovery
2026
EMANUELE GUERRINI

High flue-gas temperature may indicate an opportunity to recover useful energy. Yet an economizer cannot be sized from temperature alone. The value of recovery depends on mass flow, receiving-fluid conditions, operating hours and the constraints of the wider system.

01. Flue-gas flow and composition

Mass flow and heat capacity set the available heat. Check full-load and part-load conditions, distinguish measurements from assumptions and identify contaminants. Gas composition affects materials and maintenance.

02. Minimum permissible gas temperature

Lower is not always better. Condensation, acid dew point where relevant, stack compatibility and downstream equipment all impose limits. The acceptable minimum temperature depends on the fuel and process.

03. The heat user

Heat recovery adds value only where there is a matching demand. Feedwater, process water and air preheating have different temperature profiles and operating schedules. The cold-side temperature can materially constrain recovered duty.

04. Fouling and pressure drop

For dusty or corrosive streams, fouling can matter more than the theoretical heat-transfer coefficient. Define cleaning access, bypasses, gas velocities and available draft. Adding surface area is not automatically more economical.

05. Actual project economics

Recovered kW is not a business case on its own. Compare useful recovered energy against operating hours, displaced fuel cost, shutdowns, auxiliary power, cleaning, installed CAPEX and lifetime. Calculate payback for the real plant profile.

Bottom line. Start with a verified heat balance and constraints. Then select geometry, materials, cleaning and integration.
